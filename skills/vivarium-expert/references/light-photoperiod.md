## 12/12 is the biologically correct tropical baseline

Equatorial day length at 0 degrees latitude is a near-constant 12/12 year-round. For a SE Asian rainforest biotope, hold it there.

| Parameter | Tropical build | Drier / temperate build |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline photoperiod | 12 h on / 12 h off | 12 h, optionally 11-13 h seasonal |
| Ramp | ~30 min sunrise, ~30 min sunset | same |
| Seasonal variation | minimal (equatorial) | contested — see below |

**The seasonal-cue literature actually disagrees:**
- Husbandry-industry position: shortening days cue brumation and breeding.
- Physiology-literature position: **temperature, not photoperiod, is the dominant reproductive timing cue in reptiles**, and "many reports of photoperiodism have been questioned on the basis of inadequate experimental design."

**Practical read: if you want seasonal cycling, cycle temperature first and photoperiod second. Do not assume a light timer alone does anything.**

**Build note:** run the ramp on a controller, not a mechanical timer. Hard on/off at 100% startles animals and gives no dawn/dusk behavioural window. Most horticultural drivers do this natively; some reptile bars need an external dimmable driver.

**Sources:**
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0016648072901785 — "Environmental physiology of reptilian breeding cycles: Role of temperature"

**Unverified:** The 30-minute ramp is a commercial controller convention, not an animal-welfare study.
