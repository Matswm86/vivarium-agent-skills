## Any powered intake sharing water with shrimp gets a foam pre-filter

A bare canister or pump intake pulls in shrimp larvae and juveniles and can injure or kill adult shrimp and small crab juveniles. Fit a **fine-pore foam pre-filter sponge directly over the intake strainer** — fine-pore specifically where *Caridina* or *Neocaridina* breeding is a goal, since the juvenile stage is most at risk. This is a load-bearing safety requirement for that stocklist, not a refinement.

**Filtration options for a shallow, planted, warm paludarium pool:**

| Method | Fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sump below the cabinet** | Best if the stand has room | Largest media volume, serviceable without disturbing the display, hosts heater + ATO reservoir + dosing together; needs an overflow or gravity drain plus a return pump (can be the waterfall pump) |
| **Canister** | Good compact alternative | External, so no bulky hardware in a shallow pool; strong mechanical + biological capacity |
| Internal sponge / hang-on | Workable under ~50-80 L | Simple and cheap, limited media volume, visible unless hidden |
| Undergravel in a false bottom | Supplementary only | Common paludarium technique — false bottom filters *and* lets land-section runoff pass without waterlogging substrate; pair with a powered pump pulling from the false-bottom void |

**The practical convention for a land-dominant paludarium: a false-bottom drainage layer feeding a sump or canister.** That gives mechanical plus biological filtration, hides the heater, pump and ATO reservoir, and adds water volume that buffers parameter swings better than a shallow display pool alone.

**Turnover: 4-6x the water-section volume per hour** for a planted, lightly stocked, shrimp-safe system — lower than a fish-only tank's 8-10x, because high flow stresses shrimp and disturbs fine substrate and plantings. **Size to the actual water volume, not the enclosure footprint**, and stay at the low end with shrimp or crab juveniles present.

**Drain at build time.** Fit a bulkhead through the floor or lowest side panel at the water section's lowest point, feeding a ball valve below the cabinet. Retrofitting a bulkhead into finished glass is a specialist drilling job, not a DIY afterthought — and the pane must be **annealed**, never tempered.

**Unverified:** Turnover targets and the false-bottom convention are widely repeated hobby guidance, not tied to a single citation.
