## The precautionary "crabs eat everything" default is wrong in a specific, useful way

**The best evidence available is a first-hand 10-month trial:** 2 male cherry shrimp (*Neocaridina*) in a water section 100 x 17 x 6 cm deep, alongside **50+ vampire crabs, 10 months**. Both shrimp survived and grew to full size. The crabs made "numerous attempts to catch and eat the shrimp"; the shrimp's "exceptional speed and agility" defeated them — "**the crabs are just too slow for the shrimp.**"

The author's own caveat: crabs "eventually get lucky," preferentially taking **weak or sick** shrimp, and shrimp die of other causes more often than of crab predation. Conditions that made it work: dense cover, shallow water section, **adult** shrimp.

Commercial sources disagree ("they would most likely finish up as lunch"). Both views are shown; the trial is the only actual data.

**Compatibility table:**

| Combination | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Adult *Neocaridina* | **WORKS with caveats** | the 10-month trial; dense cover required, juveniles cropped |
| A breeding shrimp colony as a display goal | **FORBIDDEN** | crabs take weak, sick, and juvenile individuals continuously |
| Fast nano-fish (Boraras, Trigonostigma, Danionella) | **WORKS** | mechanism is speed: crabs are not aquatic hunters |
| Slow or long-finned fish, incl. domestic Betta and guppies | **FORBIDDEN** | "easily targeted by vampire crabs" |
| Adult ramshorn snails | **WORKS** | two keeper sources run them together |
| Small or juvenile snails | **RISKY** | one source cites predation risk on small snails |
| Springtails and isopods | **WORKS — recommended** | keeper guide calls springtails "super important" *in the crab enclosure* |
| Live plants | **WORKS — plant freely** | see `fauna-geosesarma-and-plants.md` |
| Frogs | **UNRESOLVED** | **zero sources either direction.** Not resolved-negative — genuinely unknown |
| A second *Geosesarma* species | **FORBIDDEN** | "only keep one species of crab together" |
