## Two different braces solve two different problems

A **euro-brace** is a continuous glass band siliconed around the inside top perimeter, tying all four panes together against outward bowing.

Trade rules:
- Tanks longer than **~1.2 m** or over **~380 L** normally need bracing.
- Brace band width **50-100 mm**, scaled with tank size.
- Brace thickness **>= wall thickness**.
- Add a **cross-brace** (front-to-back strip across the middle of the opening) on long tanks.
- Going rimless means glass thickness and corner silicone carry everything — build **one thickness step above** the braced equivalent.

**A tall paludarium needs a second, lower brace that a normal aquarium does not.** The top brace is a metre or more above the waterline and does nothing for the water column. A **waterline ledge** — 10 mm glass, 80-100 mm deep, full internal width, set ~50 mm above design water level — restores the propped-support assumption that the beta = 0.37 coefficient depends on, and doubles as the shelf the false bottom sits on. Without it, size the pane for beta = 1.0 (see `glass-paludarium-cantilever-bound.md`), a 63% thickness penalty (my calc).

**Bonding:** bond the brace flat against the pane edges with a full-width bead of the same aquarium silicone, then run a fillet along the inside junction. Do not rely on a thin skim — the brace is a tension tie and bond area carries it. Fit the brace **after** the box is square and the corner seams have cured; bracing a wet box locks in whatever error is present.

**A dry terrarium over 1.2 m is also a bracing case**, but for stiffness and safety rather than water. Fit a 60 mm top-front rail bonded to the top edges of the front returns, running the full length (also the natural mount for hinged doors or an upper door track), plus a matching rear rail. A cross-brace is usually skipped because it obstructs access. Do **not** leave a long opening with nothing but a loose mesh lid — the front and back panes bow and the corner seams see peel.
