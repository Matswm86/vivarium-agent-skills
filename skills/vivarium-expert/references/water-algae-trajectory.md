## Expect the sequence, do not panic at month two

- **Weeks 1-6:** thin **biofilm slime** (bacterial and fungal, not true algae) on constantly wet surfaces. Normal, and it is the substrate grazers later rely on.
- **Months 1-3:** **diatoms** (brown algae) bloom on new wet surfaces, especially while the system is cycling with elevated silicates. Near-universal, and typically self-limits as the system matures — provided flow and light are not excessive.
- **Months 2-6:** **green algae** takes over as diatoms recede — green spot and film, sometimes filamentous hair algae where flow and light both concentrate near the waterfall discharge.
- **Months 6-12:** a mature wet wall settles into a **mixed algae/biofilm equilibrium**: green on bright zones, biofilm and diatoms in shadier low-flow pockets. Largely cosmetic rather than dangerous, but it discolours rockwork and **can clog fine spray-bar nozzles**.

**Management, in order of effectiveness:**
1. **Grazers.** *Neocaridina* and *Caridina* graze biofilm and soft algae wherever they can reach; nerite snails are the standard answer for glass and hardscape.
2. **Cut the photoperiod** to 6-8 h/day — the single most repeated intervention in planted-tank algae control, and it slows algae without starving the plants.
3. **Design in cleaning access** — a removable rock or hinged background panel, which also serves pump and tube maintenance.
4. **Keep flow moving** rather than pooling; stagnant high-nutrient pockets are where filamentous algae take hold fastest.
5. **Choose porous, rough-textured background material.** Foam plus substrate coat, coco-fibre, or tree-fern panels give grazers surface area and grip; a smooth sealed surface is one both algae and grazers struggle to hold.

**Unverified:** This trajectory is a pattern repeated across build logs and care guides, not tied to one citation.
