## Fog is an aesthetic effect, not humidity control

Ultrasonic foggers produce room-temperature fog via a vibrating disc, marketed to raise humidity without adding heat. Two problems:

**1. Bacterial amplification is documented, not folklore.** A peer-reviewed case report documents two sporadic legionellosis cases specifically linked to domestic ultrasonic humidifiers, with epidemiological confirmation that *Legionella pneumophila* amplified in the reservoir was the probable source. It states ultrasonic humidifiers "can act as an amplification mechanism for pathogenic microorganisms when not cleaned and maintained correctly." Zoo Med's own guidance is to empty the reservoir before storage.

**If you use a fogger: RO water only, empty and dry the reservoir between uses, clean weekly.**

**2. Cold fog condenses.** Fog at room temperature entering a warmer enclosure condenses on cooler surfaces, producing standing water and permanently fogged glass rather than a true RH rise. Same dew-point physics as `climate-condensation-dew-point.md`. In a cold-climate build where the glass is already the coldest surface in the system, cold fog works against you. (**UNVERIFIED as a quantified claim** — keeper consensus plus the dew-point mechanism.)

**Verdict: use warm misting for actual humidity control. Run fog briefly at dawn on RO water if you want the look.**

**Misting schedule to start from:** 5 seconds every 3 hours while the lights are on, then tune to the **drying cycle** — leaves should dry between cycles — rather than to a hygrometer setpoint.

**Nozzle count: no published per-m3 rule exists. UNVERIFIED.** Design judgement for a ~1.7 m3 tall enclosure: 4-6 nozzles — 2-3 across the canopy for epiphytes, 1-2 at mid-height aimed at the background or dripwall, 1 low. For a small dry terrarium, 1-2 nozzles aimed at a humid retreat and cork, not the whole enclosure.

**Water quality:** ~50 micron nozzles plus hard tap water will scale. RO is standard practice for both nozzle life and glass clarity. (Vivarium-specific source: UNVERIFIED.)

**Auto top-off matters specifically here.** Misting with RO adds mineral-free water to the land section, but evaporation from the water section removes water and leaves every dissolved solid behind. Without top-off, TDS in the pond climbs steadily and the level drops until livestock are stranded or the filter intake sucks air. Feed an ATO with **RO water only** — never tap. Prefer a unit with an optical primary sensor plus a mechanical float backup and alarm over a single-float unit.
