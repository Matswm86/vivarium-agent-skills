## They do different jobs, which is why serious builds run both

A **waterfall's** evaporative surface is limited to the wetted background face and the splash zone below. It raises ambient humidity somewhat, but it is primarily a visual, audio, and water-circulation feature.

A **dripwall or misting system** spreads or atomises water across a much larger surface — moss, cork bark, leaf litter — on a timed cycle, and is the more effective humidity driver of the two. Cloud-forest builds commonly run **both**: a waterfall as the centrepiece and continuous circulation, plus misting on its own timer so humidity control is independent of whether the waterfall pump is running.

**Splash noise control** — falling water hitting a hard surface or open water is the dominant noise source:
- Angle the discharge so water runs down a **textured** (not smooth) face rather than free-falling through air; this converts a splash into sheet-flow.
- Use a diffuser plate, mesh, or lava-rock lip to break one jet into many rivulets before the drop.
- Land the water over submerged rock or a marginal-plant shelf, not into open deep water.
- Keep total flow moderate. **A slower, wider sheet is quieter than a fast narrow jet at the same volume.**

**Hiding the plumbing:** embed the return tube in the foam or coco-fibre background **during** construction, before the final texture coat — but run it through a **rigid PVC conduit sleeve** set into the foam so the tube can be pulled and replaced later without demolishing the backwall. Put the pump and its intake sponge low, hidden behind rockwork or a divider. Build a cleanout access point at the top discharge so the spray bar can be pulled for cleaning.
