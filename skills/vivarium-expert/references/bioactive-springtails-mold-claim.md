## The hobby's flagship claim has zero measurements behind it

"Springtails are the single most effective anti-mold solution" circulates everywhere. No quantitative efficacy data exists for it — not one CUC-present vs CUC-absent trial at matched humidity has ever been published.

**Wrong:**

```
Mold bloom → add more springtails, wait   # leaves surface RH untouched; overfed
                                          # springtail cultures themselves grow mold
```

**Right:**

```
Mold bloom → cycle humidity: mist, then let surfaces dry; target 30-day average
surface RH < 80% (ASHRAE 160-2021); flat 95% RH around the clock is a mold farm.
Springtails stay as detritivores and a moisture bioindicator, not as the fix.
```

**Why:** mold growth in enclosures tracks water activity and surface RH, and the only mechanism-grade collembolan fungivory study shows *Folsomia candida* preferring truffle mycorrhizal fungi in a lab at 20 C — not the *Trichoderma*/*Aspergillus*-type saprophytes that bloom in builds. A new build blooms for 2 weeks to 2 months as part of normal cycling regardless of crew (see `build-wood-selection-and-prep.md`); the bloom is microfauna food, not an emergency.

**Verify:** run the missing experiment yourself: two identical bins, same substrate and misting schedule, springtails in one. Photograph mold coverage weekly for 6 weeks.
