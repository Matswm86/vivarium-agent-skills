## Passive ventilation runs on the chimney principle

Cool room air enters through a **low front vent**, is warmed inside, rises, and leaves as warm moisture-laden air through a **high rear or top vent**. Continuous passive exchange, no draught.

**The driving force is the temperature difference between the two openings.** That is why the vents must be at *different heights*, and why a vivarium with two vents at the same level barely ventilates at all.

A vivarium-systems patent puts it directly: "Without a fan, you would need at least two vents and a difference in temperature between them to get any amount of air exchange, which is why you see one at the hotter top and one near the cooler bottom of a vivarium."

**Height helps you.** The stack effect scales with height, so a 160 cm build ventilates passively far better than a short enclosure and may need **less** vent area than intuition suggests, not more.

**On internal fans there is a real counter-view.** TerrariumQuest argues *against* internal fans, preferring more passive vent area, on the grounds that fans "don't provide any particular value that couldn't be attained by simply providing more space for air exchange." That author does use small PC-style fans, but as *exhaust* over adjustable top vents, not as internal blowers.

**Fan sizing sanity check (my calc):** a 1.68 m3 enclosure with a single 60 mm Noctua NF-A6x25 at minimum speed (16.7 m3/h) run continuously as an exhaust gives **~10 ACH** — far too much. Run it at low speed as an internal circulator, not an exhaust, or on a duty cycle of a few minutes per hour.

**PC fans in 100% RH: nobody has published corrosion data. UNVERIFIED.** Standard PC fans are not humidity-sealed; bearing and PCB corrosion is plausible and widely reported anecdotally. Mount the fan in the dry zone above the canopy or outside the enclosure blowing across a vent, conformal-coat the PCB, and treat fans as consumables.
