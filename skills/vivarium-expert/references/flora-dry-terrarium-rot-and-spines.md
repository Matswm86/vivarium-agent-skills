## Two constraints that decide the dry-terrarium plant list

**1. Misting and succulents are structurally incompatible.** "Misting gives succulents the worst of both worlds: not enough water for the roots, too much moisture on the leaves." Reptile enclosures typically need higher humidity than succulents prefer, creating exactly the conditions that cause rot.

Species-confirmed rot risk: **Gasteria** — "prone to Fusarium root rot, if they are over-watered"; **Sansevieria** — "can rot from over-watering."

Reptile-industry rule: "Most plants need to not have damp substrate touching the leaves at the base, as this causes them to rot."

**Fix, and it is a design constraint not a species choice:** either spot-mist the animal and its hide rather than the whole enclosure, or plant succulents on **raised mounds or rock outcrops so the crown never sits in damp substrate**. Even correctly chosen species (Haworthia, Gasteria, Sansevieria) rot if the misting regime wets their base routinely.

**2. Exclude spined plants from any zone the animal walks.**

| Species | Reason | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| *Dyckia* spp. | Rigid spines; explicit reptile-industry spine-avoidance rule | Background/inaccessible accent only |
| *Agave* spp. (even dwarf) | 1.5-3 cm terminal spines | Background/inaccessible accent only |
| *Ficus microcarpa* | 70-100% humidity requirement contradicts a dry floor | Exclude, or isolate in a sealed humid micro-pot |
| *Cyrtomium falcatum* | Needs evenly moist conditions | Exclude from the dry zone |
| *Crassula* (jade) | Retailers recommend it despite an ASPCA mammalian-toxicity flag; no reptile-specific data resolves it | Substitute *Portulacaria afra* — confirmed non-toxic, same visual niche |
| *Pyrrosia* spp. | UNVERIFIED; genus generally wants higher humidity than arid tanks provide | Research gap — not a "no," but not yet a "yes" |

**Toxicity health warning about the data itself:** **no dedicated reptile-specific toxicity database was reachable.** Every rating available is from the ASPCA dog/cat/horse database — a **mammalian proxy, not reptile toxicology**. Treat "non-toxic to dogs/cats" as reasonable but unverified, and "toxic to dogs/cats" as a firm reason to exclude, pending a real reptile source.

**Lighting: use two separate fixtures.** Industry-standard practice is a UVB tube for reptile health **plus** a separate PAR/grow bar for plants — not one combined bulb.
