## Insulation is the cheapest watt you will ever buy

U-values: single-pane 5-6 mm glass **U ~ 5.7 W/m2K**; double glazing with air cavity **U ~ 2.8**; 50 mm XPS at lambda 0.034 W/mK gives **U = 0.034/0.050 = 0.68 W/m2K** (my calc).

**A 150 x 70 x 160 cm build, 9.14 m2 of surface, room at 21 C** (my calc, steady-state conduction only):

| Target inside | dT | All single glass | XPS 50 mm on back+sides+base, glass front+top | Also double-glazed front/top |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 C | 4 K | 208 W | 94 W | 54 W |
| 26 C | 5 K | 260 W | 118 W | 68 W |
| 28 C | 7 K | 365 W | 165 W | 95 W |
| 27 C (room 18, night) | 9 K | 469 W | 212 W | 122 W |

**Insulating back, both sides, and base cuts demand 55-75%.** It also doubles as the condensation fix.

**Timing is the trap: XPS cannot be retrofitted once epiphytes are mounted on the background.** Insulate before building the background, not after.

**Credit the lighting.** 120-140 W of LED inside the box is 120-140 W of heat during the photoperiod. On an insulated build that covers most or all of daytime demand — **the real heating problem is the night, not the day.** The thermostat must handle a large day/night swing in demand.

**Vertical gradient is free.** Warm air rises, so a 160 cm column self-stratifies: warm at the top for arboreal baskers, cooler and more humid at the substrate and water surface for amphibians and shade plants. Design the animals into the strata rather than fighting the physics.
