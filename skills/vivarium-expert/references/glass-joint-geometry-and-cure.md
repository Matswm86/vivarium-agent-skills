## Silicone is a flexible structural adhesive, not a glue line

**Wrong:**

```
Press the panes together hard so the joint is as thin as possible
```

**Right:**

```
2 x 2 x 2 mm plastic spacers every ~200 mm along each joint before closing,
left buried in the silicone permanently
```

**Why:** A bead thinner than ~1 mm has almost no movement capability. Differential expansion between panes and any glass flexure is then taken as strain concentration at the glass interface rather than distributed through the sealant.

| Parameter | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum bond thickness | **1 mm** | Soudal Silirub AQ TDS |
| Practical pane gap | 2-3 mm | DIY practice, not a standard |
| Bead width rule | bead width ~= glass thickness | UNVERIFIED against primary DIN 32622 text |
| Internal fillet | triangular fillet in every inside corner, additional to the butt bead | as above |

**Cure schedule:**

| Milestone | Figure | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Tack-free | ~30-45 min | general 1-part silicone TDS, not the aquarium SKUs |
| Cure-through rate | **2-3 mm per 24 h** at 23 C / 50% RH | GE TDS; industry range 1.5-5 mm/24 h |
| Before loading / leak test | **48-72 h at 20 C** | terraristikshop.net |
| Conservative alternative | 2 weeks before adding water | aquariumscience.org |
| Leak test | fill completely, hold **>= 24 h**; run a dry paper towel along every seam for micro-seepage | aquarium.tools |
| Before livestock | no manufacturer figure exists — **UNVERIFIED** | — |

**Recommended schedule (judgement, stated as such):** 7 days cure in a warm ventilated room, 48 h dry structural check, 72 h water leak test on a tiled floor with the tank on its final stand, drain and refill, run filtration and plants 2-3 weeks, then livestock. A 3 mm bead at 2-3 mm/24 h is nominally through-cured in 24-36 h, but fillets are thicker than the bead.

**Repairs: silicone does not stick to silicone.** Cured silicone is chemically inert; fresh silicone over it forms a weak interface that leaks under hydrostatic pressure. Strip back to bare glass with a razor blade, clean mechanically then with isopropanol or acetone, then apply fresh. Never bead over old.
