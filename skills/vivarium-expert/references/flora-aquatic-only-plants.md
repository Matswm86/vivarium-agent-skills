## Two common mis-specifications

**Riccia fluitans is an aquatic plant, not groundcover.** It "must be somewhere in an enclosure that has sufficient amounts of water," is normally grown fully underwater or pinned partially submerged, and is "not suitable for emersed growth, as it dries out quickly outside water."

**Fix:** pin it to mesh or hardscape underwater, or float it at the waterline. For dry terrestrial floor cover use a true terrestrial liverwort or moss (*Riccardia*, *Fissidens*).

**Utricularia graminifolia is intermediate-to-advanced, not a beginner carpet.** It is "one of the easiest plants to keep **if certain conditions are met**," but "sudden, aggressive changes in water parameters can also trigger a massive melt," and it "does best with CO2 injection and high lighting." Multiple independent sources converge on intermediate-to-advanced, melt-prone on tank immaturity and ammonia spikes.

**Fix:** either add CO2 to the aquatic zone specifically for this plant, or substitute *U. sandersonii* or *U. livida*, which are not flagged as CO2-dependent in the same way.

**Cryptocoryne melt is expected, not a failure.** Commercially emersed-grown crypts melt when submersed as they redirect energy into new submersed-form leaves; established crypts also melt from "water quality shifts, lighting changes, temperature fluctuations, fertilizer adjustments, CO2 injection variations, tank relocation."

**Fix:** buy stock already grown in the target form where possible. Plant once and keep the aquatic chemistry stable afterwards (this also protects *U. graminifolia*). Trim melting leaves at the substrate to prevent ammonia and rot. **Do not panic-remove melting crypts — check root health first.** Regrowth follows in 3-4 weeks under stable conditions if roots stay healthy.

**Adiantum (maidenhair fern) is the opposite case** — often called too delicate, but a **stable 80-100% RH enclosure is exactly what it wants**. The risk is not ambient humidity but **drafts near vent sections and door-opening zones**. Keep it away from ventilation points rather than out of the tank.
