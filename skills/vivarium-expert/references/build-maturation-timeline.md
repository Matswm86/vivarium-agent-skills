## Two plant classes, two completely different planning rules

**Sourced growth rates:**

| Plant | Rate |
|---|---|
| Anubias (all species) | "produce a leaf every 3 weeks, or even slower"; *A. barteri* var. *nana* ~1 leaf/month, var. *barteri* ~1 leaf/3 months |
| Bucephalandra | "slow growth"; mature size 2-60 cm species-dependent, creeping rooting stems |
| Tillandsia | epiphytic, "may take months or years to flower" — visible maturity is a multi-year event |
| Neoregelia | epiphytic, roots are "primarily hold-fasts," nutrients via leaf litter and rainfall in the central cup — no numeric rate found, **UNVERIFIED** |
| Moss (general) | untended surfaces "will typically be colonised within a few years" by spore-fall alone; deliberately mounted moss establishes far faster |

**The planning rule that follows:**

- **Plant hardscape-mounted slow growers (Anubias, Bucephalandra, moss) at a density that looks intentionally sparse at month 1**, because they genuinely fill in.
- **Mount near-static-size epiphytes (Tillandsia, Neoregelia, orchids) at close to their true mature footprint from day one**, because they will not meaningfully change scale on any timeline relevant to the first two years.

**Month 1:** hardscape is the entire visual story. Foliage is thin and clearly just-planted; moss mounts are discrete patches, not continuous texture. **Plan for this stage rather than apologising for it** — it is exactly why getting the hardscape shape right matters more than initial planting density.

**Month 6:** moss slurry and thread mounts knit into continuous mats. Bucephalandra and Anubias add roughly 4-8 leaves each over six months at the sourced rates — visible thickening, still identifiably young. Tillandsia and Neoregelia are essentially unchanged.

**Year 2:** moss has fully colonised its mounts and begun opportunistic spread onto adjacent surfaces, giving the uneven crevice-favouring texture that is the goal look. Anubias and Bucephalandra have thickened into genuine cover rather than individual plants. Epiphytes have had time to flower at least once. This is when the theme reads as convincing rather than aspirational.

**The trap: do not fake year-2 density on day one by over-planting.** That directly produces the clutter and uniformity failure modes.
