## Floating bottom for water, solid bottom for dry

| Arrangement | Load path | Silicone loaded in |
|---|---|---|
| **Walls on top of the bottom pane** ("solid bottom") | wall weight + water weight -> bottom pane -> stand | tension + shear; stress concentration at bottom pane edges |
| **Bottom pane inside the walls** ("floating bottom") | wall weight -> wall bottom edges -> stand directly; water weight -> bottom pane -> stand | **shear only**; bottom pane carries no wall load |

**For large water builds (~700 L and up), use the floating bottom.** The bottom pane does not carry side-panel weight, and silicone is stronger in shear than in tension. Concretely: at 400 mm water a 1500 x 700 build's bottom carries ~403 kg of water plus substrate and hardscape (my calc) — do not additionally hang ~175 kg of wall glass off its edge seams.

**For dry terraria, use the solid bottom.** The load is trivial, it is simpler to square, it gives a flush external base, and it eliminates the internal step where substrate and frass collect. This is standard for dry vivaria.

**External dimensions change with the choice.** For a floating bottom the bottom pane is cut to (W - 2t) x (D - 2t): for a 1500 x 700 build in 12 mm, that is **1476 x 676 mm**. Order it 1-2 mm undersize so it drops in with a silicone gap on all four sides.
