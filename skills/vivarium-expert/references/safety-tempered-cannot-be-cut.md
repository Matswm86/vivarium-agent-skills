## Every hole goes on the cut list before tempering

Tempering is irreversible. The process locks compressive stress into the glass surface; cutting, drilling, or notching breaches that layer and releases the stored energy, shattering the whole pane at once.

**Wrong:**

```
Order tempered panes -> build -> "I need a bulkhead for the drain"
-> drill -> pane detonates
```

**Right:**

```
Final dimensioned cut list INCLUDING every hole, notch and cutout
-> then tempering. Order any pane that will be drilled as ANNEALED.
```

**Why:** There is no second chance and no repair. Additionally, tempered glass can fail **spontaneously** from nickel-sulphide inclusions, with no impact at all — which is why it is the right choice for doors a person might fall against (it dices into blunt fragments) and the wrong choice for a pane retaining hundreds of litres of water.

Glass type selection:

| Type | Strength | Failure mode | Cut/drill after? | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Annealed float | MOR ~19-45 MPa by convention | large sharp shards, slow crack growth | **Yes** | anything drilled: tank bottoms, bulkhead panes |
| Toughened (ESG) | ~4x annealed | dices small and blunt; can fail spontaneously | **No** | doors, panes someone could fall against |
| Laminated (VSG) | as annealed per ply | cracks but the PVB interlayer retains the water | No | best safety choice for a large viewing pane |
| Low-iron (Optiwhite, Diamant, Starphire) | **same as normal float** | as annealed | as annealed | viewing panes, for clarity only |

**Low-iron changes clarity, not strength.** Ferric oxide is reduced to ~0.01% versus roughly 10x that in ordinary float, giving up to ~8% more light transmission. It "does not affect the structural integrity or safety rating of the glass." At 12 mm, ordinary float shows a strong green cast in the sightline.

**Verify:** View a pane through polarised sunglasses. Tempered shows rainbow or geometric stress patterns; annealed shows nothing.

**Ordering checklist:**
1. Dimensioned cut list with every hole, notch, and cutout, before toughening.
2. Drilled panes specified **annealed**, explicitly.
3. Edge finish specified: ground and arrised (seamed) minimum, polished on exposed rims. EN 16612 penalises unpolished edges in design strength, and sharp edges are both a laceration hazard and a crack initiation site.
4. Low-iron specified by trade name on viewing panes.
5. Order a spare front pane if budget allows — breaking it during assembly is the most likely schedule disaster.
