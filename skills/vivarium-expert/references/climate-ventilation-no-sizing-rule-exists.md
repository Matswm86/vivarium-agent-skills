## The number circulating on the internet is from the wrong species

**Wrong:**

```
"Vivariums need 10-15 air changes per hour."
```

That figure is the **NIH laboratory animal-holding-room standard for ventilated rodent caging.** It is completely inapplicable to a sealed, 80-100% RH glass paludarium. Applied to a 1.68 m3 enclosure it would evaporate roughly **7 L/day** into the room (my calc).

**Right:**

```
A high-humidity paludarium wants roughly 0.5-2 ACH.
```

**And that estimate is mine, not a citation — UNVERIFIED.**

**The honest state of the literature: no cm2-per-litre or percent-of-footprint vent-sizing rule exists in any authoritative source.** This was searched specifically and not found. TerrariumQuest's ventilation page gives no numeric sizing rule. Frogfather's 2026 guide gives **zero numeric specifications** for vent dimensions, mesh-to-glass ratio, ACH, or fan specs, deliberately preferring observational criteria.

**The one numeric rule found anywhere:** a lid roughly **2/3 sealed glass and 1/3 open screen** works well for dart frogs — stated for the US Mid-Atlantic to upper South East climate.

**Climate correction that matters:** that rule was derived in a humid US region. In a **cold-climate winter with 20-25% room RH the humidity gradient across the vent is far steeper, so the same vent area loses much more water.** Expect to need a *smaller* open fraction — start at 1/4 to 1/5 open, and **make it adjustable** (a sliding glass panel over the screen section) so you can open up in summer and close down in deep winter.

**Verify by behaviour, not by number.** The best guidance actually found is observational: "fresh-smelling air, healthy plant growth and limited long-term condensation." Leaves should dry between misting cycles.
