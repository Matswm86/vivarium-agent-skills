## 70 cm of depth is an opportunity most tanks do not get

A standard high-end aquascape depth is closer to 45-50 cm, so 70 cm stages real front-to-back layering. The failure mode is the same principle in reverse: put anything large-scaled at the back and the depth cue vanishes.

**Techniques:**

- **Leaf-size gradient.** Small-leaved plants (Bucephalandra, mosses, small ferns) at the rear against larger-leaved plants in the foreground exaggerates distance beyond the physical depth. Bucephalandra ranges "from tiny specimens at 2 cm to larger ones reaching 60 cm" species-dependent — the small end is the useful rear-planting material.
- **Hardscape-size gradient.** Larger branch and root diameter in the front third, progressively thinner branches and smaller stones toward the back. **The rear pieces need to be genuinely smaller, not just further away** — an identically scaled piece repeated at the rear destroys the illusion.
- **Path vanishing lines.** A stream or dripwall path that narrows as it recedes, in both visible width and the gap between flanking hardscape. At 70 cm depth this needs only a few centimetres of taper to read at a 1-1.5 m viewing distance.
- **Rear darkening.** Lower light intensity and a darker backing at the rear third pushes it back — the eye reads darker and hazier as further away, which is why real atmospheric haze and cloud-forest mist do the same thing at scale. A dark board (black or deep green/blue, never a bright poster backdrop) also gives waterfall spray something dark to be lit against.
- **Layered strata.** Foreground low carpet and small leaf; midground medium ferns and mid-height epiphytes; background tallest planting and densest moss mass. Same understory/midstory/canopy logic as a real forest.

**Depth budget for 70 cm (my synthesis, not a sourced ratio):** front 0-20 cm foreground detail and viewing clearance; 20-50 cm midground with the main planted mass and biggest wood diameter; 50-70 cm background with smallest leaf and wood scale, darkest lighting, rearmost mist source.

**Compose for the actual viewing position.** Golden-ratio and triangular placement are calculated relative to the primary viewing axis. A tall enclosure is often viewed standing and in passing rather than seated dead-centre — re-check the focal point and vanishing lines against the **habitual** viewing position, not the geometric front-centre.

**Route equipment into the rear zone.** Misting nozzles, fogger lines, heater cables, pump intakes, and sensors break both the biotope illusion and the negative-space argument if they sit in the primary sightline. The rear third is already the darkest, least-detailed part of the composition and the natural place for the eye not to look. **Equipment routing is a compositional decision as much as an engineering one.**
