## Hunger and overcrowding, not preference

**Baseline:** isopods are detritivores, "built to eat decaying matter, not healthy living tissue"; in almost all setups they leave plants alone and improve the soil.

**The condition that flips it:** "If you have a large colony and not enough food, isopods will turn to whatever is available — and that can include tender living plant tissue, seedlings or delicate roots. **It's driven by hunger and overcrowding, not preference.**"

**The one species-specific warning:** "there have been various reports about ***Cubaris murina* eating plants (particularly ferns)**, so perhaps not the best species to introduce to your exotic terrarium setups."

**Use it as a diagnostic:** "If isopods start eating moss or live plants, it is usually a sign that something is wrong" — a response to missing nutrients, not random behaviour.

**Verdict:**
- *Cubaris* as a genus is **not** a blanket plant risk. Hunger and overcrowding are the risk.
- **Avoid *C. murina* specifically in a fern-heavy enclosure.**
- Keep supplemental protein and vegetable food constant. **Plant damage is the alarm that the CUC is under-fed, and the correct response is to feed it, not to remove it.**
