## Four requirements, four different materials

A tall wet wall must simultaneously (1) hold its bond to glass through years of permanent wetting, (2) carry plumbing plus the weight of saturated moss and mounted plants, (3) give root-penetrable anchor points at every height, and (4) leach nothing into water holding shrimp. No single method does all four.

1. **Waterproof shell** — epoxy (Pond Shield class) at **>=10 mils** over the substructure, or a purpose-built waterproof back panel. This protects the glass-silicone bond from years of standing moisture. Some epiphyte panel systems (EpiWeb-IIS) explicitly *require* a waterproof backing.
2. **Waterfall channel** — a pond-rated closed-cell foam, used as specified: bonding stone without mortar and forming a water-shedding skin that directs water **over, not under**, rocks. This is the one place paying ~3x over standard foam is justified, and it is the manufacturer's stated use case.
3. **Epiphyte layer** — a synthetic epiphyte panel (EpiWeb-IIS with a pump rated to the wall height) cut into the planted zones. UV-stable, non-decomposing, pH-neutral, root-penetrable, and it solves irrigation for a tall vertical face in one product. Tree fern does the same botanical job at roughly **$310/m2** with CITES-constrained and shrinking supply.
4. **Texture and structure** — cork tile and flats plus the foam + silicone + coco method in the drier upper and lateral zones.

**Do not use carved styrofoam + cement** on a soft-water build: the multi-week leach protocol and permanent efflorescence risk fight directly against a low-KH target (see `build-rock-carbonate-test.md`).

**Do not rely on moulded rock panels as the primary wall** on a deep build: they consume 12-15 cm of depth, reptile-grade panels float, and a sealed face gives epiphytes nothing to root into.

**Foam cure gates:** pond foam cures in ~8 h at a 25 mm bead and misting speeds it, but the practical gates are **12 h before carving, 24 h before proceeding** — the skin hardens in 30-60 min while the interior stays pliable. A European gun-grade PU foam gives skin formation 10 min, cutting time 25 min (EN 17333-3), and is explicitly **"Not UV-resistant."**

**UV is the failure mode for PU foam, not water:** "If left exposed, the foam will eventually crumble." Keep the silicone or coco skin unbroken.
