## An insectivore in a bioactive tank competes with the bioactive part

**Lepidodactylus lugubris (mourning gecko)**, 8.5-10 cm total length (4-4.4 cm SVL), all-female parthenogenetic, 1-2 eggs every 4-6 weeks. Documented diet includes insects, spiders, amphipods, **pill bugs (isopods)**, fruit, nectar, pollen, and its own eggs.

**That is a direct, sourced conflict with a springtail-and-isopod clean-up crew** — independent of any other compatibility question in the tank.

***Phelsuma* (day geckos)**, genus spanning ~6.5-30 cm total length. Wild diet is insects and invertebrates plus nectar, pollen, and soft fruit; captive diet is fruit flies and crickets. **A day gecko sharing an enclosure with a fruit-fly-fed dart frog culture competes for the same food at minimum, and may predate small frogs.** Unconfirmed, but a real risk to check before mixing.

**The general rule this illustrates: before adding any insectivore to a bioactive enclosure, look up what it actually eats in the wild, not just its size.** A species entry with no incompatibility listed has not been researched.
