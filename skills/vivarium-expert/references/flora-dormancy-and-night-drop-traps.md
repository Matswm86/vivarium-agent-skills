## A stable warm enclosure is a dormancy trap

**Highland Nepenthes need a night drop your tank will not give.** Highland care sheets specify day "high 70s to high 80s F," night "mid 40s to upper 50s F" — an **8-12 C+ drop**. Even lowland Nepenthes "expect some temperature drop at night," but lowland day range is 84-88 F with temperatures "rarely falling below 70 F" and no strict night-drop demand.

**Fix:** drop highland species entirely. Use lowland ecotypes only, verified per species before purchase. Hanging above a waterfall is the right placement — the species selection is what needs correcting, not the position.

**True Sarracenia is temperate and needs a winter dormancy.** Not workable in a permanently warm tank. Drop it.

**Temperate Drosera has the same problem, plus a light problem.** "Carnivorous plants in the wild often grow under full sun," 10-16 h direct sun outdoors, and plants in dim light "collapse faster than a balloon on a cactus." Tropical non-dormant sundews (*D. capensis*, *D. aliciae*, *D. spatulata*) "do not require a cold winter dormancy and can be grown year-round under warm conditions" with 4-6 h+ direct sun or a dedicated ~2500-lumen LED spot.

**Fix:** use only tropical non-dormant Drosera, and give that bog section **its own dedicated high-output spot bypassing the canopy shade.** This is a lighting-engineering fix, not just a placement fix.

**Sphagnum is the counter-intuitive case.** A tropical grower reports live Sphagnum "does just fine in 39 C afternoons for extended periods" — the actual failure mode is **humidity fluctuation plus air movement, not heat**. Red-pigmented cultivars (*S. capillifolium*) need cool nights (<=20 C) to hold colour, which a stable-warm tank will not provide. Use a green species. Treat as promising, not proven: single-source, one grower's account.
