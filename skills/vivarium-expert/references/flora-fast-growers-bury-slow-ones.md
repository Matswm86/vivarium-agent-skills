## The competition is inside the tank, not outside it

**Ficus pumila (creeping fig)** is "fast-growing… can eventually grow up to 15 feet in length" and "will most likely need frequent trimmings to keep it from becoming too large and taking over the whole enclosure." No exact cm/month figure was sourced.

**Rule: usable only with a committed monthly pruning schedule confined to one background panel. Do not let it share a wall with slow epiphytes (Bulbophyllum, jewel orchids) that it will physically bury within a year.** The reduced-vigour cultivar 'Minima' gives tighter control (growth-rate comparison UNVERIFIED).

**Selaginella kraussiana** carries invasive listings — New Zealand's National Pest Plant Accord, and the EPPO Database of Alien Plants in Ireland (weed / quarantine weed / garden escape / environmental weed; introduced 1955, now spread over 1,900+ km2).

**Read that correctly.** That is invasiveness in the **outdoor** sense; it will not escape a sealed glass enclosure. **The real implication is inside the tank:** expect it to outcompete slower groundcover (*Fissidens*, *Peperomia prostrata*, *Ludisia*) if uncontained. Use physical barriers — rock walls, wood dividers — or commit to a trimming schedule. Do not let it share open floor space with anything slow you want to keep visible long term.
