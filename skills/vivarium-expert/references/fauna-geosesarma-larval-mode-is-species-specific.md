## Buy on the trait, not the genus name

The hobby repeats that *Geosesarma* are direct developers that breed entirely in fresh water. The primary literature says the genus is **split**, and names the exception.

- *Geosesarma mirum*: "The development of *G. mirum* is direct (i.e., abbreviated)"; females "observed brooding young crabs under the pleon"; eggs ca. 1.0 mm.
- ***Geosesarma hednon* "has small eggs and planktotrophic larvae."** Planktotrophic means free-swimming feeding larvae — **not** direct development.
- Genus egg size averages **1.2-1.8 mm**. Large egg = direct development; *G. hednon*'s small egg is the tell.

**The rule to apply when buying:** large eggs (>1.2 mm) **and** a female seen brooding *crabs*, not eggs, under the pleon. Genus membership alone guarantees nothing.

**Confirmed direct developers among trade species:**
- *G. dennerle* (Ng, Schubart & Lukhaup 2015, Raffles Bull. Zool. 62:3-13, Central Java): "fully formed miniature versions of the adults hatch from the eggs." Female carries **20-80 eggs**, ~1 month incubation, juveniles 1-2 mm at hatch, sexual maturity ~6 months.
- **Cannibalism begins once brood care ends** — juveniles need dense cover or separation.

**Still unresolved:** larval mode for *G. hagen*, *G. notophorum*, and the "Red Devil" / "Vampire" trade morphs. Do not assume.
